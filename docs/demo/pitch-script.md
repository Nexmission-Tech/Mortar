# Mortar Pitch Script

The talk track for the Chin Hin preliminary pitch in October 2026: an online
call with a shared screen and a live demo, in front of the sales department and
top management. It grew from the Kabel DXP check-in on 29 September 2026.

- **Deck:** [mortar-pitch-deck.html](mortar-pitch-deck.html), eleven slides.
  Press `N` for speaker notes; the [PDF](mortar-pitch-deck.pdf) is the backup.
- **Live App:** [mortar-d18f.onrender.com](https://mortar-d18f.onrender.com), on
  simulated data only.
- **Length:** about 22 minutes, then questions. One case and its named owner are
  on screen inside two minutes; the slides carry the business case; the live
  walkthrough opens on the Sales Chart and ends on a recorded SPA signing.
- **Online:** share one browser tab at 1440 × 900 or larger. Keep the camera
  still while you talk, move it only to frame a block, and switch to the Grid if
  the shared screen stutters.

Speak to the room, not the slide. Every slide carries one idea; the lines below
add to it rather than read it.

## Slide Talk Track

### 1. Cover — A, 45 Seconds

**Say:** "We are Team NexTechnologies, and this is Mortar. One sentence: a
booking is a promise, and only a signed SPA is a sale. Mortar shows who should
act next and lets staff assign dated work; the pilot tests whether that produces
more timely SPAs. Before the slides, one booking."

### Live Glimpse: One Case — C, 1 Minute

The room sees a named owner before it hears a single claim about ownership.
Mortar AI's part comes later, on slide 6.

**Do:** as Tan Mei Ling (Loan Admin), on her Bookings, search **A-12-03** and
open BK-9001: Waiting On names Apex Bank, and the Next Step names Tan Mei Ling.

**Say:** "This is BK-9001, booked 16 days ago. Apex Bank is still deciding, and
its banker has just asked for the latest payslips. Tan Mei Ling in Loan Admin
owns the next move — the case names her — and Nurul coordinates the booking.
Hold that picture: the next twenty minutes are how it got there, and what we
have not proved yet."

### 2. The Business — A, 1:15

**Say:** "Start with Chin Hin, not with us. Chin Hin Group Property reported
RM2.2 billion of unbilled sales at 30 June. Those are signed SPAs waiting to be
billed as construction moves, so every ringgit of it passed the gate we care
about: booking to signed SPA. The brief says a stalled booking can hold a unit
off the market for six to twelve weeks, and REHDA puts loan rejections for
RM500,000 to RM700,000 homes at 38 percent. The brief's sharpest line is that
nobody in the group can say how many live bookings will sign."

**Do Not Say:** that unbilled sales are unsigned bookings, or multiply them by
any conversion rate.

### 3. The Workflow Today — A, 1:15

**Say:** "This is the workflow from the brief and our survey. Sales books and
chases documents, Loan Admin works the bank, Legal gets the SPA drafted and
signed. The red lines are where bookings die. Status lives in a CRM,
spreadsheets, WhatsApp, email and property software. So a file can read 'with
bank' for weeks when the real next move is a buyer's missing payslip, and nobody
is assigned to it."

**Land:** the gap is ownership, not information.

### 4. Evidence And Assumptions — B, 1:30

**Say:** "We keep what we know apart from what we assume. Seven of eight
practitioners we surveyed name loan rejection as the single biggest cause, five
of eight say bookings stall at bank credit assessment, and seven of eight call
an early financing check the most helpful change. REHDA's 38 percent comes from
a published industry survey. On the right are four assumptions, each with the
pilot test that would prove it wrong. Eight people rank causes; they do not
measure Chin Hin."

**Do Not Say:** "87.5 percent", or that seven of eight Chin Hin bookings fail on
financing.

### 5. Solution Logic — B, 1:15

**Say:** "Our logic in five steps. A banker's message is pasted in. Mortar AI
reads it and proposes a payslip request. The black step is the rule: a person
confirms, and only confirmed evidence moves the case. The blocker becomes a task
with a named owner and a date — for a document the bank wants, that is Loan
Admin — and the forecast counts expected signings, not bookings."

**Land:** AI proposes; people confirm; every blocker gets an owner.

### 6. Meet Mortar AI — B, 2 Minutes

**Say:** "Mortar AI does two jobs: it reads, and it answers."

**Then, How It Reads:** "Most AI you have seen writes paragraphs. Mortar AI
chooses from a fixed list: what happened, which document, and who acts next. It
shows how sure it is, calibrated against how often it is right. Below 60
percent, the proposal goes to Needs Review for a person. Unlike a general
chatbot, it cannot invent a status. This is the exact proposal the live app
shows for BK-9001."

**Then, Why It Helps:** "Follow-up is slow when staff have to spot every change.
Mortar AI reads buyer, banker and lawyer messages, Manglish included, inside
three seconds, with a cached answer if needed. Staff paste the message; Mortar
AI proposes the update, and staff check it, including which bank it updates,
before confirming. It also suggests the next step on each Today card, ranks case
playbooks and flags buyers who may be going quiet. We built it this way because
updates should be checkable, not eloquent."

**Then, How It Answers:** "In Ask Mortar AI, it answers questions only from the
staff member's authorised bookings, using five read-only tools. It cites the
booking, can read a bank-letter photo and never changes a record. Unlike a
general chatbot, it stays within the cases staff may see; if it is down, a
scripted answer steps in."

**Land:** Mortar AI picks from a fixed list and cannot invent a status; it
answers only from authorised bookings and cannot change records; a person still
confirms. The answer on the slide is worded from BK-9001's case page, not
captured live, so do not call it a live answer. If someone asks what runs it,
use the Q&A answer "What Runs Mortar AI?".

### 8. Before And After — C, 1 Minute

**Say:** "Same booking, before and after. Before Mortar, a booking like BK-9001

With Mortar, the case says who waits on whom, Tan Mei Ling in Loan Admin owns

Finance reads expected signings with a range. What we do not claim: the bank

### 9. Adoption — A, 1:45

**Say:** "Adoption starts small, and your launch queue does not change. Keep
taking bookings through your existing process. Admins import them into Mortar
once the sheet's required fields are there. A row missing one, like gross
monthly income, is held back until it is filled in. Mortar never stops you
taking a booking, and the financing-risk flag only advises follow-up. Day one,
upload the booking sheet: seven columns, matched loosely to whatever exports it
today. Each desk opens its own queue, and bankers and lawyers change nothing
because staff paste their messages in. Then twelve weeks on one project with one
Sales Admin: a baseline, one admin, a live cohort beside ordinary follow-up, and
an audit with Legal. One number judges it: the 30-day verified SPA rate, with
cancellations kept in the count."

**Show, Once:** switch to the app as Nurul Aina and open **Add Bookings**. It
opens on **Upload A Sheet**; point at **What The Sheet Needs**, including gross
monthly income, then switch back. **Demo Entry**, the other tab, fills in
made-up buyer details and is for demos only.

**Land:** inside the brief's rules: no new CRM, no consultant, no vendor, and no
gate in front of taking a booking.

### 10. Technology Second — B, 1 Minute

it, an anonymised booking export with the unit register, and two practitioner
interviews. Thank you. Any of us can take any question."

**Land:** the data boundary comes before the asks, never after them.

### Figures Bridge

Rehearse this once, under the Manager profile and the desks' today (18 September
2026), before quoting any figure. To a finance controller, 148, 123, 64, 59, 50
and 21 on different screens look like populations that do not tie; this sentence
ties them:

> "Of 148 recorded bookings, 25 exited, so 123 stand: 64 signed and 59 open. Of
> the 59 open, 50 are inside the day-30 estimate and 9 are older. Their 50
> chances add up to 20.8 expected signings."

- **148 Recorded Bookings:** every booking in the demo, 140 generated and eight
  hand-written stories.
- **25 Exited:** recorded cancellations and lapses; Forecast's **Lost Bookings**
  lists them. Open it once before any conversion figure.
- **123 Units Booked:** the Sales Chart's first figure, the bookings still
  standing. It is not total intake.
- **64 SPA Signed:** confirmed signings among the 123.
- **59 Open:** unsigned and not exited; 50 are under 30 days from booking, 9 are
  older and stay in the queues.
- **20.8 Expected Signings:** the sum of the 50 bookings' day-30 chances, not a
  new count. Say "about 21", and never "21 bookings will sign".

No calculation changes between screens; only the population named does.

## Questions To Expect

Short answers, spoken in the first person plural. The basis matters: say
"evidence", "our assumption" or "what the prototype does" when asked.

**Q:** Where does "Available" come from?

**A:** In the prototype, from the project layout in Settings: blocks, floors and
units a floor. In a pilot it comes from your unit register, imported beside the
bookings. Without a register we would show a unit as unknown, never as
available.

**Q:** Is the 3D view just decoration?

**A:** It is the same data as the grid next to it: every unit, its state, and a
click that opens the case. The building is the sales chart your team already
reads; the Grid is there for keyboards, small screens and anyone who prefers it.

**Q:** Can one salesperson see another's units?

**A:** They see every unit's state, so the whole team reads the same tower. The
buyer and the case open only on the desk that holds the booking; anyone else
sees whose desk to ask.

**Q:** What is Release Review?

**A:** A list for a person's decision, never an action. It lights a booking
where the buyer withdrew, two banks rejected with nothing live, or a month
passed with no live application. Today it is four bookings, RM 2.939m at full
booking prices, 19 to 56 days since booking: not a loss, not cash and not stock
days saved. A rejected case opens on its Financing Review, because two
rejections do not prove recovery is exhausted. An authorised person decides
whether to release the unit. Mortar never cancels a booking.

**Q:** What happens the day a bank rejects, a buyer hesitates or an appointment
is missed?

**A:** Each reaches a desk without waiting for a week of silence. A confirmed
rejection that leaves no bank deciding, or a confirmed valuation shortfall, puts
a Financing Review on Loan Admin's Today the same day. A confirmed buyer concern
makes Call The Buyer the Sales Admin's step until the call's outcome is
recorded. An SPA appointment that passes unsigned shows as SPA Appointment
Missed on Legal's Today the next working day, first in the list.

**Q:** Do you block a booking for financing risk?

**A:** No. Keep taking bookings through your existing process. Admins import
them into Mortar once the sheet's required fields are there, gross monthly
income among them. The risk flag only advises follow-up; the bank makes the
credit decision.

**Q:** Does the Booking Owner picker show which agency's buyers sign?

**A:** No. Mortar shows units by recorded owner; it is not an agency conversion
table. The picker highlights units, and the figures keep their scope.

**Q:** Does Stalled Booking Value mean that person lost the money?

**A:** No. It is the full booking price of stalled bookings whose next move sits
with that person. The named person owns the chase, not necessarily the delay,
and the figure is not a loss estimate.

**Q:** Why are some floors 3A, 13A and 23A?

**A:** Many Malaysian developers skip the number four. The project settings
choose skip-four or plain numbering, and Add Bookings checks each unit against
it.

**Q:** Chin Hin is rolling out Kingdee. Why add another tool?

**A:** We are not replacing a system of record. Mortar is the follow-up
discipline between desks: who owes the next move and what evidence closed it.
The pilot tests that on one project and feeds whichever system holds the master
booking record.

**Q:** What does this problem cost Chin Hin?

**A:** Units held off the market for weeks, marketing and legal time spent on
bookings that fail, and a forecast built on gross bookings. We cannot quote Chin
Hin's own leakage rate: no public figure exists, and pilot weeks one and two
rebuild it from closed files.

**Q:** Does seven of eight mean Chin Hin loses most bookings to financing?

**A:** No. Eight self-selected practitioners in mixed roles. It ranks what to
investigate first; it is not Chin Hin's cause share.

**Q:** What happens when someone clicks Create Task?

**A:** An assigned task with an owner and a due date lands on that person's
queue. It does not message the buyer or the bank. Completing it is a close-out:
one line on what happened, then either the update recorded or a next date. "No
answer, try Tuesday" leaves a dated follow-up on the same desk, so the booking
stays on Today. A Manager's follow-up never reads Answered from a ticked box,
only from a later confirmed update.

**Q:** Who confirmed that update, and when did it happen?

**A:** The case's History shows both dates, Occurred On and Recorded On, so a
month-end cut-off is checkable: an SPA signed on 30 September but recorded on 3
October shows both. It names who reported the update and who confirmed it. It is
a staff-confirmed record; Legal's execution audit is separate.

**Q:** Do buyers, bankers or lawyers need to adopt anything?

**A:** No. The developer has no authority over them, so staff paste their
messages in and confirm what they say.

**Q:** Can the AI approve a loan or move a case on its own?

**A:** No. Mortar AI's reading is a proposal; only a person's confirmation moves
the case, and below 0.60 confidence it is flagged Needs Review.

**Q:** What Runs Mortar AI?

**A:** Only if asked, and in one breath: two engines behind one name. Reading
runs on TypeSafe's Jev, which returns typed choices with a probability that code
can check and a person can confirm. Answering runs on Google's Gemini, which
writes a sentence, only from the asker's bookings and through five read-only
tools. Staff only ever see Mortar AI.

**Q:** Why not just use ChatGPT for everything?

**A:** A chatbot writes free text that someone still has to interpret. Mortar AI
answers fixed questions with a calibrated confidence, so the software can check
the answer, route it to the right desk and hold it for a person. For turning a
message into a case update, that is the whole job. Answering a person's question
in a sentence is the one place it writes.

**Q:** What does Mortar AI actually do?

**A:** Four jobs, each a handful of fixed questions: read a message into an
event, a document and an owner; pick the next move for a stalled case; rank our
staff playbooks for a case; and score how responsive or hesitant a buyer is. It
has three seconds per call, and its answers are cached, so pages never wait on
it.

**Q:** What if the AI is wrong, or down?

**A:** Staff dispute or dismiss the proposal and record the right event by hand.
If reading is down it falls back to a cached or stale answer, or none; if
answering is down, Ask Mortar AI answers from counts of the same bookings. Pages
never wait on either.

**Q:** Is the forecast AI?

**A:** No, arithmetic. For each live booking we take the share of similar past
cases, at the same stage and age, that signed within 30 days, and add them up.
Every rate shows its sample size and range.

**Q:** How often was the forecast right?

**A:** Point at it, as Robert Khoo: Forecast, **Signing Outlook**, **How This Is
Counted**. The Manager's view holds every booking; a Loan or Legal view drops a
booking as it signs, so it does not show the check. The accuracy check runs the
same method at 19 August, from what was recorded by then: it expected 9.9
signings by booking day 30, and 10 happened. It was over-confident in the 50–75%
band, 54% expected against 40% that happened, from only ten bookings. That is
simulated history, not measured on Chin Hin data.

**Q:** Where is the one number, the 30-day verified SPA rate?

**A:** Forecast, **Conversion And Turnaround**, **Cohort Result**. Pick an
intake's booking dates and it reads, for example, "11 of 25 signed by booking
day 30 (44%)" for 23 July to 19 August, with cancellations and lapses kept in
the count and the estimate kept out of it. In a pilot, Legal audits each signing

**Q:** Are the What To Do playbooks your bank and legal policy?

**A:** No. Each one reads Illustrative, Not Yet Reviewed, and keeps its action

**Q:** Is the sign-in real security?

**A:** The profile picker is a demo convenience, but the server enforces access
through a signed session: each profile sees only its own bookings. Production
needs proper single sign-on.

**Q:** Can we load real buyer data next week?

**A:** Not yet. Everything today is simulated. Real data needs agreed identity,
processing and retention terms, and Mortar AI's answering engine runs on a free
tier we restrict to simulated data.

**Q:** What does it cost to run?

**A:** Today, one free Render service and a free database: proof the mechanism

are not costed yet.

**Q:** Why 30 days?

**A:** A horizon we chose so a cohort can be enrolled and read within one
quarter. It measures a contract secured, not cash received.

**Q:** Where does "eight extra SPAs" come from?

**A:** Arithmetic: 40 stalled bookings times a 20-point lift. Every input is an
assumption the pilot replaces; it is not a result.

**Q:** What is hard about the pilot?

**A:** The comparison, not the software: enrolling a cohort, keeping a
comparable group on ordinary follow-up, and agreeing who is eligible before we
start.

**Q:** What do you need from us?

**A:** A pilot owner, the source of booking and signing evidence, agreement on
the comparison group, and which system is the master booking record.

## Do Not Say

- That receiving a document approves a loan or recovers a sale. It clears one
  document blocker; the bank still decides.
- That completing a task verifies a milestone. A milestone needs its own
  confirmed event.
- That we increased conversion, saved inventory days or brought cash forward.
  The pilot produces those numbers.
- That IFCA is Chin Hin's system. One survey respondent suggested it; Kingdee is
  the announced ERP programme.
- That Mortar sends WhatsApp messages. Staff paste messages in; Mortar never
  sends anything.
- That the forecast is AI. It is arithmetic on stage rates, with a range.
- That a signed SPA means cash received, revenue earned or commission payable.
  Say "SPA execution is the conversion milestone we measure".
- That Available means unsold in Chin Hin's own system. It comes from the
  prototype's layout; a pilot imports the unit register.
- That Release Review cancels anything, or that two rejections mean release. A
  person decides; banks apply their own credit policies.
- That a Release Review or Stalled Booking Value figure is a loss, cash or stock
  days saved. Every money figure there is full booking prices.
- That the named follow-up owner caused the delay. They own the chase.
- That the seven Loan Approved, No SPA Appointment bookings only need a signing
  date. They have recorded approval and no appointment; Legal checks what still
  blocks signing.
- That Mortar makes sure every booking has a named person until it signs. It
  shows who should act next and lets staff assign dated work; the pilot tests
  whether that produces more timely SPAs.
- That nobody retypes anything, or that one click confirms an update. Staff
  paste the message and check Mortar AI's proposal, bank included, before
  confirming.
- That Mortar sees a booking before the sheet's required fields exist, or that
  it gates a booking on financing risk.
- That the Booking Owner picker ranks agencies or salespeople by conversion.
- That the evidence has a name on it as proof of execution. It is a
  staff-confirmed record; Legal's execution audit is separate.
- That every unsigned day costs a set amount in late-delivery damages.
- That Mortar AI and Ask Mortar AI are two separate AIs. Mortar AI reads and
  answers; Ask Mortar AI is the panel where staff ask.
