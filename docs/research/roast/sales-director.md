# Mortar: A Sales Director’s Roast

## First Impression

I recognise the problem: we celebrate the launch, then my people spend weeks
finding out whose desk the sale died on. Your named next step is useful, but
telling me “booked is not sold” does not earn you another hour of my admins’
day. Show me one booking moving, tell me who does the extra work, and keep your
income form away from my launch queue.

## The Roast

Rank means influence on my pilot decision. **Already Raised** refers to the
existing operations review; those findings are acknowledged, not added to the
backlog again. Figures below are simulated, and the main booking and forecast
counts were reproduced from the demo rules.

The sponsor’s balance matters: “The challenge is not to make one team the
problem, but to find a practical way to address both perspectives.” Sales needs
booking momentum; Finance needs a credible view of conversion. Neither gets that
from renaming a booking a sale.

## Report

**Pilot Verdict:** I would back a bounded, one-project pilot in principle,
subject to the five conditions above. Today I would authorise the simulated
workflow rehearsal; the pitch itself says real buyer-data use is not ready. The
named chase owner and evidence trail earn that trial, but no measured commercial
uplift earns a rollout yet.

The three questions Mortar answers worst are **what quarterly improvement it
causes**, **which bookings will sign this calendar month**, and **how much staff
time the process consumes**. Each lacks the evidence needed for a firm
commercial answer; all three limitations were already raised.

**Verification Limits:** I inspected the supplied screenshots, pitch, deck,
product material and current rules, and ran read-only checks of demo counts,
document ownership, release review and income validation. I did not exercise the
live browser, observe staff at work or verify real signing outcomes, forecast
accuracy, agency performance or external company figures. The requested
root-level readme was absent; I read the relocated project overview instead, and
the full project check could not finish because the TypeScript compiler was
unavailable.

**Next Action:** Rehearse the first two minutes: take one unit from the Sales
Chart to its actual named next-step owner.
