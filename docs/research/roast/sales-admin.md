# Mortar: A Sales Administration Executive’s Roast

## First Impression

The blocker and named next step are useful; I can stop rebuilding the whole file
from three WhatsApp chats whenever the director calls. But your quickest booking
form supplies made-up buyer finances, and your booking search can hide my own
unit because it has reached Legal. After eight years at this desk, I will give
you a trial, but I am keeping Excel open until I trust what a hurried click
records.

## The Roast

Rank means how much the answer affects my pilot decision. This is a desk-role
walkthrough of the supplied screens and current rules, not an observed morning
with Chin Hin staff. **Already Raised** names the operations review or earlier
roasts; those gaps do not become fresh recommendations here.

The sponsor asks for “something a Sales Administration Executive could actually
use in their day-to-day work”. Sales needs to keep taking bookings without being
blamed for bank decisions; Finance needs to stop treating those bookings as
certain sales. I am the person who must keep the shared record current while
both teams ask questions, so extra recording work matters.

**Desk Effort Check:** As an illustration, ten straightforward buyer messages
from already-open full cases use 40 workflow-button clicks. Five buyer-call
records use another 20 control clicks: open Record An Update, open What
Happened, select Buyer Contacted, save. That is **60 clicks**, ten message
pastes and five notes before case navigation, input focus, date corrections,
reading or the calls themselves; the numbers describe a chosen workload, not an
observed day or a time saving. Excel still wins the single-cell edit; Mortar
earns its place only if the shared blocker, owner and history reduce repeat
status enquiries and duplicate chasing.

## Report

**Pilot Verdict:** Yes to a supervised, one-project rehearsal with synthetic
bookings today; no to making this build my sole live daily tracker. The
real-data boundary is **Already Raised — Finance Controller, Rank 5**.

The three questions Mortar answers worst are **which bank a hurried confirmation
changes and how to reverse it** (Rank 1), **who covers me on leave** (Rank 4),
and **whether this costs less staff time than Excel** (Rank 5). Reversal and
delegation remain verbal limitations; staff effort still needs observation.

The sponsor’s **How Do You Know It Worked?** test requires “one number that can
be read within one quarter”. Keep **30-Day Verified SPA Rate**, including
cancelled and lapsed bookings in the eligible denominator, and mark improvement
**Not Yet Measured**. Forecast estimates and task ticks do not prove that
outcome: **Already Raised — Operations Gap 8**.

**Verification Limits:** Screens, supplied documents and code were inspected;
isolated rule checks reproduced the unit, permissions, intake and proposal
findings. Live Mortar AI accuracy, browser timings, real workbook compatibility,
staff effort and business outcomes remain unverified. The root project overview
was absent; I read its relocated copy. Full checks stopped at the missing
TypeScript compiler; the formatter was also unavailable.

**Next Action:** Spend two minutes rehearsing Rank 1 with two synthetic bank
applications: identify the proposed bank, dismiss the wrong proposal and record
the correct bank’s update by hand.
